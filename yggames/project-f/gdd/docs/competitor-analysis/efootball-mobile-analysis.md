# eFootball Mobile -- Competitor Analysis

**Game:** eFootball (formerly PES Mobile / Winning Eleven)
**Developer/Publisher:** Konami Digital Entertainment (NetEase for China)
**Engine:** Unreal Engine 4
**Price Model:** Free-to-Play with Microtransactions
**Platforms:** Android, iOS, Windows, PS4, PS5, Xbox One, Xbox Series X/S, Nintendo Switch 2 (Summer 2026)
**Analysis Date:** March 2026

---

## 1. Overview & Evolution

### History
- **Origins:** eFootball is the successor to Konami's legendary Pro Evolution Soccer (PES) franchise, known as Winning Eleven in Japan. The PES series dominated football gaming in the early 2000s, particularly PES 5 and PES 6.
- **PES Mobile (2017-2021):** Konami released PES Mobile as a premium-quality mobile football game. It earned a devoted following for its superior on-pitch gameplay compared to FIFA Mobile. The mobile version was a standalone app but shared DNA with the console game.
- **Rebrand to eFootball (2021):** In July 2021, Konami announced PES was being renamed "eFootball" and restructured as a free-to-play, cross-platform live-service game. This was a seismic shift -- from annual $60 releases to a unified F2P platform.
- **Mobile Launch:** The mobile version of eFootball launched on June 2, 2022, replacing the beloved PES Mobile.
- **Annual Seasons:** The game receives annual season updates rather than new releases:
  - 2022 Season: September 30, 2021 (console/PC) / June 2, 2022 (mobile)
  - 2023 Season: August 25, 2022
  - 2024 Season: September 7, 2023
  - 2025 Season: September 12, 2024
  - 2026 Season: August 14, 2025
- **Nintendo Switch 2:** A spin-off called "eFootball Kick Off!" is planned for Nintendo Switch 2 in Summer 2026, signaling platform expansion.

### Player Base & Ratings
- The PES/eFootball franchise has historically had hundreds of millions of downloads across mobile platforms globally.
- **Metacritic (eFootball 2024):** User score of 3.7/10 (Generally Unfavorable) based on 135 ratings -- 58% negative, 22% mixed, 20% positive. This reflects the console version; mobile sentiment tends to be slightly more favorable.
- **App Store Ratings:** Typically 3.5-4.0 stars on Google Play and Apple App Store, dragged down by server issues and bugs but buoyed by gameplay quality.
- The game is particularly popular in Asia (Japan, South Korea, Southeast Asia), South America, and parts of Europe.

### Key Licensing Partners
- FC Barcelona, Manchester United FC, FC Bayern Munchen, AC Milan, Inter Milan, Arsenal, Liverpool FC
- National team licenses for various federations
- Exclusive legends: Messi, Neymar, Luis Suarez (featured in trailers), Platini, Rummenigge, Lothar Matthaus, Roberto Donadoni, Demetrio Albertini
- **30th Anniversary (2025-2026):** The franchise celebrated its 30th anniversary with special events, collaboration kits, and legendary player releases.
- **FIFAe World Cup 2026:** Konami secured the official FIFAe World Cup 2026 esports partnership, a major competitive legitimacy boost.

## 2. Core Gameplay

### Match Mechanics
- **Simulation-Focused:** eFootball aims for realistic, physics-based football simulation. Player weight, momentum, and ball physics all factor into gameplay. This is its core differentiator from the more arcade-like FIFA Mobile.
- **Unreal Engine 4:** The game uses UE4, delivering higher graphical fidelity than most mobile football games, particularly in player models and stadium rendering.
- **Console Parity:** Unlike FIFA Mobile (which is a completely different game from the console EA FC), eFootball mobile shares the same core engine and gameplay logic as its console/PC counterpart. This is a unique selling point -- mobile players get a "real" football game experience.
- **Cross-Platform Play:** Crossplay was added in a major April 2025 update, allowing mobile players to compete against console/PC players. AI opponents were also added in this update.
- **Match Duration:** Matches are typically shortened (6-10 minutes real-time) to suit mobile sessions.

### Controls
- **Touch Controls:** Default for mobile. Virtual joystick on left, action buttons on right. Simplified compared to controller input but functional.
- **Classic Controls:** For experienced players who want more manual control over passing, shooting, and dribbling.
- **Smart Assist:** A significant accessibility feature that Konami has invested heavily in:
  - **Attacking:** Auto-selects optimal pass targets based on defensive positioning; automatically adjusts pass type (lofted, through ball); dribbling assist helps avoid nearby opponents; shooting power/trajectory is auto-determined.
  - **Defending:** Auto-clears in dangerous situations; Match-up command makes defenders automatically position to block passes/shots; defenders auto-crouch and take precise positioning steps.
  - **Toggle:** Can be enabled/disabled via Extras > Game Settings > Play Settings > Smart Assist.
- **Controller Support (Mobile):** The mobile version supports external controller input, further bridging the gap between mobile and console experience.

### Game Modes
1. **Authentic Team:** Play with real licensed club and national teams. Available in:
   - Offline vs AI
   - Local multiplayer
   - Online user matches (events and friendlies)
2. **Dream Team:** The primary mode and monetization driver. Build an original team by:
   - Hiring coaches and managers
   - Signing players through various contract mechanisms
   - Customizing formation, team name, uniforms, and playing style
   - Playing both online and offline through the Events section
3. **Events:** Rotating online and offline challenges with specific rewards (training programs, currencies, player contracts).
4. **Campaign Hub:** A structured set of objectives and missions that guide player progression.
5. **Line Objectives:** A noughts-and-crosses (tic-tac-toe) style reward system where completing lines of objectives unlocks bonuses.
6. **Master League (DLC):** Konami sells Master League and other offline career modes as paid DLC -- a controversial decision given the F2P model. This is primarily a console/PC feature.

## 3. Card/Player System

### Player Card Types (Tier Hierarchy)
| Card Type | Description | Rarity |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| **Epic** | Ultimate tier players -- peak versions of legendary footballers | Highest |
| **Legendary** | Retired football legends (Rummenigge, Matthaus, Platini, Donadoni, Albertini, etc.) | Very High |
| **Trending** | Players performing exceptionally in real-life current season | High |
| **Highlight** | Spotlighted players from specific events or leagues | High |
| **Featured** | Enhanced versions of current players with boosted stats | Medium-High |
| **Standard** | Base versions of all current professional players | Base |

### Player Development System
eFootball has one of the deepest player development systems in mobile football:

1. **Level Training:** Use "Level Training Programs" (earned as event rewards) to grant Experience Points. Players can also gain XP by being featured in matches.
2. **Player Progression:** When a player levels up, they earn "Progression Points." Players can allocate these points in two ways:
   - **Auto-allocation** based on the player's natural growth pattern
   - **Manual allocation** to customize the player's stat development (e.g., making a midfielder more defensive or offensive)
3. **Skill Training:** Use "Skill Training Programs" to add up to 5 additional skills to a player. This allows deep customization of player abilities.
4. **Position Training:** Use "Position Training Programs" to increase a player's proficiency in up to 2 additional positions (assigned at random from eligible positions).
5. **Player Fusion:** Transfer Experience Points and Additional Player Skills from one player card to another. This prevents total waste when replacing players.

### Playstyles & Coaching
- **Manager Coaching Affinity:** Managers have specific tactical setups and coaching styles that affect team performance.
- **Formation Customization:** Full control over formation shape, player positioning, and tactical instructions.
- **Playing Styles:** Each player has inherent playstyles (e.g., Goal Poacher, Build Up, Box-to-Box, Anchor Man, Creative Playmaker, etc.) that determine AI-controlled movement and positioning.
- **Playstyle Synergy:** Players perform better when their playstyle matches the team's tactical setup and manager's coaching affinity.

### Nominating Contracts & Signing Mechanisms
- **Chance Deal:** Sign a random player from a curated player list (gacha element).
- **Nominating Contract:** Sign a specific player of your choosing from a curated list (reduces RNG, more player-friendly).
- **Selection Contract:** Sign a player of your choice from an exclusive Player List.
- **Standard Player List:** Browse and sign any base player using filters. Welcome rewards can be used here.
- **Special Player List:** Access to in-form players, league-featured players, and football legends.
- **Standard Player Tickets:** Free tickets earned through events; sign random players.
- **Packs:** Bundles containing players, managers, strips, and other items.

## 4. Monetization Model

### Virtual Currencies (Three-Currency System)
| Currency | How to Obtain | What It Buys |
|----------|---------------|--------------|
| **eFootball Coins** | Purchase with real money OR earn in-game | Player signings, in-game items, Special Player Lists |
| **GP (Game Points)** | Earned by playing matches and meeting in-game conditions | Player signings, manager signings, basic items |
| **eFootball Points** | Earned in-game + out-of-game campaigns (loyalty program) | Player signings, exchange for in-game items |

### Monetization Mechanisms
1. **eFootball Coin Packs:** Direct purchase of premium currency with real money. Coin prices vary by region, with bundles offering bonus coins at higher price points.
2. **Special Player Lists (Gacha/Agent Draws):** The primary monetization driver. Weekly rotating lists of enhanced players (Featured, Trending, Legendary, Epic). Players spend Coins to "draw" from these lists. Odds are disclosed per regional regulations.
3. **Breakthrough Pass:** A season-pass-like mechanism offering exclusive players and rewards. Example: "Breakthrough Pass B" featuring Rummenigge (87 CF) and Matthaus (87 CMF).
4. **Nominating Contracts (Targeted Acquisition):** More expensive but guaranteed -- pay to select a specific player rather than relying on RNG.
5. **Manager Signings:** Managers with different tactical setups can be acquired through GP or Coins.
6. **Master League DLC:** Sold as paid DLC on console/PC, not a mobile-specific monetization.

### Pack Odds & Transparency
- Konami discloses draw probabilities as required by platform regulations (Apple/Google) and regional laws (Japan's gacha regulations).
- Typical featured/legendary player odds are low (similar to industry standard gacha games), with most draws yielding standard-tier players.
- The Nominating Contract and Selection Contract systems partially mitigate pure gacha frustration by offering deterministic acquisition paths.

## 5. Pay-to-Win (P2W) Analysis

### How Much Does Spending Matter?

**Assessment: Moderate P2W with Skill Emphasis**

**Arguments FOR P2W:**
- **Player Quality Gap:** Epic and Legendary players have objectively higher stats than Standard players. A full team of maxed Epic/Legendary cards will outperform a F2P standard squad in raw numbers.
- **Faster Progression:** Paying players access top-tier players immediately rather than grinding events over weeks/months.
- **Featured Player Rotation:** The best versions of popular players rotate weekly, creating FOMO pressure. Missing a featured Mbappe or Messi means waiting months for another chance.
- **IGN's 2022 Review** specifically criticized the pay-to-win model, noting it as a core issue alongside gameplay problems.
- **Metacritic User Reviews:** Multiple users note that "special players bought with money are just insane" and create unfair advantages.

**Arguments AGAINST P2W (Skill Matters):**
- **Gameplay Skill Ceiling:** eFootball's simulation-style gameplay has a high skill ceiling. A skilled player with a mediocre squad can beat a whale with poor skills. Dribbling, passing vision, defensive positioning, and tactical understanding all require genuine skill.
- **Generous F2P Economy:** The game gives substantial free Coins, GP, and player tickets through events, objectives, and login rewards. F2P players report being able to build competitive 92+ overall teams without spending.
- **Metacritic User Reviews Confirm:** One user states "I have a team with all 92+ overall players and I haven't paid one cent. Honestly it may be too easy."
- **No Hard Paywalls:** All game modes are accessible without payment. There is no energy system gating gameplay.
- **Matchmaking:** Rating-based matchmaking means whales primarily face other whales at high ranks, while F2P players compete in their own bracket.

**Compared to FIFA Mobile:**
- eFootball is **less P2W** than FIFA Mobile/EA FC Mobile. FIFA Mobile's OVR-based matchmaking and full seasonal resets create a stronger pay-to-compete dynamic.
- eFootball allows players to keep their investments across seasons (no full reset), meaning F2P players can accumulate competitive squads over time.

### Verdict
eFootball sits in a "pay-to-accelerate" zone rather than hard P2W. Spending gets you better players faster, but the simulation-heavy gameplay means player skill remains the primary differentiator. The gap between F2P and paid is real but narrower than in FIFA Mobile.

## 6. Season/Reset Mechanics

### eFootball's Approach: Soft Carry-Over (Key Differentiator)
- **No Full Reset:** Unlike FIFA Mobile (which historically wiped all player progress each season), eFootball does NOT reset player squads between seasons. Your players, their levels, skills, and progression carry over.
- **Season Transitions:** When a new season launches (e.g., 2025 to 2026), the following happens:
  - Player stats may be updated to reflect real-world performance changes
  - New card tiers and featured players are introduced
  - Some expired event rewards or temporary items may be removed
  - Core squad and progression remain intact
- **Contract System:** Players require contracts to participate in matches. Contracts are consumed per match and must be renewed using GP (free currency). This creates an ongoing resource drain but is manageable for active players.

### Comparison to FIFA Mobile
| Aspect | eFootball | FIFA Mobile / EA FC Mobile |
|--------|-----------|---------------------------|
| **Season Reset** | No full reset; players carry over | Historically full reset each season (changed in FIFA Mobile 23 to no reset) |
| **Player Investment** | Long-term; training/progression persists | Previously wiped annually; now persists |
| **New Season Pressure** | Low -- existing teams remain viable | Previously high -- forced to rebuild |
| **Player Retention Impact** | Positive -- rewards loyalty | Previously negative -- punished investment |

**Note:** FIFA Mobile 23 (the final FIFA-branded version) actually adopted eFootball's approach and stopped resetting squads. This validates eFootball's no-reset model as the superior strategy for player retention.

## 7. Player Retention & Churn Analysis

### What Keeps Players
1. **Gameplay Quality:** The core on-pitch simulation is widely regarded as the best available on mobile. Players who value realistic football stick with eFootball.
2. **No Full Resets:** Players feel their time investment is respected.
3. **Deep Player Development:** The progression/training system gives long-term goals.
4. **Console Parity:** The feeling of playing a "real" football game on mobile.
5. **Legends & Nostalgia:** Classic players (Rummenigge, Matthaus, Platini) appeal to older football fans.
6. **Esports Legitimacy:** The FIFAe World Cup 2026 partnership adds competitive credibility.
7. **Free-to-Play Generosity:** Regular free Coins and player rewards.

### What Frustrates Players (Churn Drivers)
1. **Server Issues:** Persistent complaints about lag, disconnections, and server instability, especially during peak events.
2. **Null Results / Cheating:** Online matches voided ("nulled") by opponents who disconnect intentionally to avoid losses. This is the single most complained-about issue in the community. Konami has struggled to implement effective anti-cheat.
3. **Bugs & Glitches:** Display issues (e.g., "Special Player Lists Display Issue" reported March 2026), gameplay inconsistencies, and UI problems.
4. **Frequent Maintenance:** The community regularly encounters maintenance windows, including unscheduled "Additional Maintenance" periods.
5. **Limited Licensing:** While eFootball has key club partnerships, it lacks the breadth of EA's licensing. Missing leagues and generic team names (e.g., "Piemonte BN" instead of Juventus historically, though this has changed) frustrate some users.
6. **Cumbersome Menus/UI:** Consistently criticized across reviews. The menu system is unintuitive and slow.
7. **eFootball 2022 Launch Trauma:** Many players who left during the disastrous 2022 launch never returned. The game's reputation still carries this stigma.
8. **Content Drought:** Between major events, the game can feel repetitive with limited new content.

### Community Sentiment (r/pesmobile -- 2025-2026)
- The subreddit r/pesmobile remains active but has a love-hate dynamic.
- Top posts include QR codes for live events, memes about Konami's decisions, and petitions for changes.
- A notable post: "PETITION TO MAKE THIS IMAGE AS THE SUBREDDIT PFP. This bumass company has crossed all [limits]" -- indicating vocal frustration with Konami's management.
- Community rules explicitly prohibit "Cheating, Hacking, or Exploit Discussion," suggesting these are prevalent enough to require moderation.
- Account trading/selling is "zero tolerance" policy, indicating a real-money secondary market exists.

## 8. Strengths

1. **Best-in-Class Mobile Gameplay:** The simulation-style gameplay is genuinely the best on mobile. Physics, player movement, and ball control feel authentic. This is eFootball's primary moat.
2. **Console Parity:** Same engine on mobile as console/PC. No other mobile football game offers this. Cross-platform play (added 2025) deepens this advantage.
3. **No Full Seasonal Resets:** Respects player time investment. This was a major competitive advantage over FIFA Mobile until FIFA Mobile 23 copied the approach.
4. **Exclusive Licensing:** Partnerships with Barcelona, Manchester United, Bayern Munich, AC Milan, Inter Milan provide authentic content. Messi/Neymar/Suarez partnerships are high-value.
5. **Deep Player Development:** The level/progression/skill/position training system is more RPG-like and engaging than FIFA Mobile's simpler upgrade system.
6. **F2P Viability:** Genuinely possible to build competitive teams without spending. Generous event rewards and Nominating Contracts reduce gacha frustration.
7. **Esports Credibility:** FIFAe World Cup 2026 partnership legitimizes the game as a serious competitive platform.
8. **30th Anniversary Legacy:** The Winning Eleven/PES brand carries enormous nostalgia value, especially in Asia and South America.
9. **Controller Support on Mobile:** Unique feature that appeals to hardcore players.
10. **Smart Assist Accessibility:** Lowers the barrier to entry without dumbing down the core experience.

## 9. Weaknesses & Mistakes

### The eFootball 2022 Launch Disaster
- **What Happened:** eFootball 2022 launched on September 30, 2021 in a catastrophically broken state.
- **Graphics:** Widely mocked as "atrocious." Bizarre facial animations went viral as memes. Player models looked worse than the PS2-era PES games.
- **Gameplay:** Laggy engine, finicky controls, dense and dull AI.
- **Content:** Launched with almost no content -- no game modes beyond basic exhibition matches.
- **Steam Reception:** Received 92% negative reviews, making it the **worst-rated game in Steam history** at the time.
- **Metacritic:** Became the **lowest-rated game of 2021** on Metacritic.
  - GamesRadar+: 1.5/5 stars
  - PC Gamer: 30/100
  - IGN (post-1.0 update): 4/10
- **Konami's Response:** Issued a public apology and promised improvements. A major "1.0" update was released in 2022 to address core issues.
- **Lasting Damage:** Despite significant improvements in subsequent seasons (2023-2026), the game has never fully recovered its reputation. Many PES loyalists permanently abandoned the franchise.

### Ongoing Issues
1. **Null Results / Cheating:** The most persistent and damaging ongoing problem. Players exploit disconnection mechanics to void losses. Konami's anti-cheat has been insufficient.
2. **Server Instability:** Lag, disconnections, and maintenance windows remain regular occurrences. March 2026 saw multiple unscheduled maintenance periods.
3. **UI/UX Design:** Menus are consistently described as "cumbersome" across all reviews. Navigation is slow and unintuitive.
4. **Licensing Gaps:** Despite strong club partnerships, eFootball lacks the comprehensive league licensing that EA FC offers. Many leagues use generic names.
5. **Limited Game Modes:** Beyond Dream Team and Authentic Team, there is little variety. No equivalent to FIFA Mobile's various sub-modes.
6. **Slow Improvement Cadence:** Each season brings only incremental improvements. Critics note that yearly updates feel like patches rather than meaningful evolution.
7. **Master League Paywall:** Locking the beloved Master League mode behind paid DLC in a free-to-play game frustrated the community.
8. **Mobile-Specific Issues:** Touch controls, while functional, can feel imprecise compared to controller input. The crossplay feature (2025) created concerns about competitive fairness between input methods.

## 10. Sustainability Analysis

### Long-Term Viability Assessment: MODERATE-POSITIVE

**Positive Indicators:**
- **Konami's Commitment:** The franchise is now in its 30th year. Konami continues annual season updates and is expanding to Nintendo Switch 2 (2026). The FIFAe partnership signals long-term investment.
- **Cross-Platform Strategy:** Unifying mobile, console, and PC under one game reduces development costs while maximizing reach.
- **F2P Revenue Model:** The live-service model generates recurring revenue through rotating featured players and seasonal content.
- **Asian Market Strength:** eFootball has a strong foothold in Japan, South Korea, and Southeast Asia -- markets where mobile gaming revenue is highest.
- **NetEase Partnership (China):** The Chinese version published by NetEase targets the world's largest mobile gaming market.
- **No Direct Competitor with Console Parity:** No other mobile football game offers the same engine as its console counterpart. This is a defensible moat.

**Risk Factors:**
- **Reputation Damage:** The 2022 launch disaster permanently scarred the brand. Rebuilding trust has been slow and incomplete.
- **EA FC Dominance:** EA Sports FC (formerly FIFA) dominates football gaming globally in both mindshare and revenue. eFootball is firmly the #2 and the gap remains significant.
- **Konami's Track Record:** Konami has a mixed reputation in gaming, with controversies around employee treatment, franchise mismanagement (see: Metal Gear, Silent Hill), and mobile-first pivots.
- **Declining Console Interest:** If console/PC player counts decline, mobile becomes the primary revenue driver -- but mobile football is an increasingly crowded market.
- **Cheating/Null Result Problem:** If Konami cannot solve the cheating problem, competitive players will continue to churn.

### Revenue Model Sustainability
The three-currency system (Coins, GP, Points) with weekly rotating featured players creates consistent spending incentives without hard paywalls. The Breakthrough Pass adds a predictable revenue stream. However, the relatively F2P-friendly economy means average revenue per user (ARPU) may be lower than more aggressive competitors.

### Konami's Strategic Position
Konami appears committed to eFootball as a flagship franchise. The 30th anniversary celebrations, FIFAe World Cup partnership, and Switch 2 expansion all indicate ongoing investment. However, the pace of improvement remains frustratingly slow for the community. The game needs a breakthrough moment -- a major update that dramatically improves quality of life, anti-cheat, and content depth -- to recapture lost players and attract new ones.

## Key Takeaways for Project F

### What to Learn From eFootball
1. **Console-quality gameplay on mobile is achievable and valued** -- but execution must be flawless at launch.
2. **No full seasonal resets** is the correct approach for player retention. Even FIFA Mobile eventually adopted this.
3. **Nominating Contracts (deterministic acquisition)** alongside gacha reduces frustration and improves monetization perception.
4. **Deep player development (progression points, skill training, position training)** creates meaningful long-term engagement loops.
5. **Smart Assist / accessibility controls** are essential for broadening the player base without alienating hardcore players.
6. **Anti-cheat is non-negotiable.** eFootball's null result problem is its biggest ongoing failure and a cautionary tale.
7. **UI/UX matters enormously.** Even great gameplay cannot overcome terrible menus and navigation.
8. **Never launch before ready.** The eFootball 2022 disaster proves that first impressions in F2P are nearly impossible to overcome.
9. **Cross-platform play is increasingly expected** but must account for input method fairness.
10. **Three-currency systems work** but the free currency (GP) must be generous enough that F2P players feel respected.

### eFootball's Core Paradox
eFootball has arguably the best football gameplay on any platform -- certainly on mobile -- yet it consistently underperforms in reviews, revenue, and community satisfaction due to everything surrounding that gameplay: UI, servers, anti-cheat, content depth, and the lasting stain of its botched 2022 launch. This is a powerful lesson: **great core mechanics are necessary but not sufficient for success.**
