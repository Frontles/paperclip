# GOLEY - Comprehensive Competitor Analysis
## Turkish MMO Football Card Game (2013-2018)

---

## 1. OVERVIEW

### What is Goley?
Goley was a 3D MMO football game developed by **Netmarble** (South Korean developer) and published in Turkey by **Joygame** (Turkey's leading online game publisher, a subsidiary of Netmarble EMEA). The game was developed in collaboration with Joygame and **CJ E&M**. It was marketed as "Turkey's first real MMO football game" and positioned as a free-to-play competitor to FIFA and PES.

### Platform History
- **Primary Platform**: Windows PC (client-based download)
- **Mobile Version**: "Football Strike!" (package: com.netmarbletr.goley) by Netmarble EMEA — an Android mobile adaptation that was later removed from Google Play
- **Facebook Version**: A Facebook-integrated version also existed and was shut down
- **Not on Steam**: The game used its own launcher/client downloaded from Joygame's website

### Key Dates
| Event | Date |
|-------|------|
| Closed Beta Launch | November 28, 2013 |
| Open Beta Launch | December 19, 2013 |
| In-app purchases disabled | July 16, 2018 |
| Shutdown announcement (Instagram) | July 19, 2018 |
| Permanent server closure (Turkey) | August 17, 2018 |
| Korean servers closure | Before Turkish closure |

### Developer/Publisher
- **Developer**: Netmarble (South Korea)
- **Publisher (Turkey)**: Joygame (Netmarble EMEA subsidiary)
- **Collaboration**: CJ E&M

### Player Base Estimates
- No exact MAU/DAU figures publicly available
- The game was popular enough in Turkey to be mentioned alongside FIFA and PES
- Active community on Joygame forums, Technopat, Eksi Sozluk, and Donanim Arsivi
- Football Strike mobile version had approximately 797 ratings on Google Play with a 4/5 average rating before removal
- Nostalgic community discussions post-shutdown indicate a sizable Turkish player base, particularly among younger demographics (school-age players)

### Ratings
- **Football Strike (Mobile)**: 4/5 on Google Play (797 ratings) — before removal
- **PC Version**: Generally well-received at launch, declining sentiment over time
- **Sikayetvar (Complaint Platform)**: Multiple unresolved complaints about Joygame/Goley; Joygame never created a brand profile and never responded to complaints

## 2. CORE GAMEPLAY

### Match Mechanics
- **3D Real-Time Matches**: Games played on a 3D football pitch with real-time action
- **Online Multiplayer**: Thousands of players online simultaneously
- **Turkish Commentary**: Famous Turkish football commentator **Sabri Ugan** provided in-game commentary — a major selling point and beloved feature
- **Licensed Players**: World-famous licensed football stars from European leagues (2005-2012 era players)
- **Team Selection**: Players chose a real team and gained access to all players who played for that team between 2005-2012
- **Character Style**: Stylized "chibi" characters without noses that players found endearing/sympathetic

### Controls
- Intuitive pickup-and-play controls
- Special moves included: Scissor Kicks, Marseille Turns, Flip-Flaps, Step-Overs
- Arcade-style football with multiplayer emphasis
- Manager-level control over Formation, Offense, and Defense strategy

### Game Modes (5 Total)
1. **Training Camp (Hazirlik Kampi)**: Practice and learn the game
2. **Single League (Tekli Lig)**: Solo competitive league play
3. **Friendship Mode (Dostluk Modu)**: Casual matches with friends, customizable room settings
4. **Ranked Matches (Rutbe Maclari)**: Competitive ranked play with matchmaking
5. **Training (Antrenman)**: Skill development mode
6. **Leader Mode (Lider Modu)**: Added later — matched players based on "Leader Points," ranked weekly with rewards for top positions. No customizable room settings (unlike Friendship mode)
7. **Champions League**: Tournament-style competitive mode

### Goal Celebrations
- Unique short animations for each famous athlete
- Players noted that Goley's entertaining goal celebrations made even conceding goals enjoyable — a standout feature vs. FIFA/PES

## 3. CARD/PLAYER SYSTEM

### Card Categories (4 Types)
1. **Football Player Cards (Futbolcu Kartlari)**: The core cards representing players
2. **Technical Staff Cards (Teknik Ekip Kartlari)**: Coaches/managers that boost team performance
3. **Equipment Cards (Ekipman Kartlari)**: Gear that enhances player abilities
4. **Emblems (Amblemler)**: Contain offensive, defensive, power, speed, technique, and mind abilities

### Card Tier System (5 Levels, ascending)
| Tier | Turkish Name | English | Color-Coded |
|------|-------------|---------|-------------|
| 1 | Normal | Normal | Yes |
| 2 | Ozel | Special | Yes |
| 3 | Ender | Rare | Yes |
| 4 | Uzman | Expert | Yes |
| 5 | Super | Super/Legendary | Yes |

**Note**: A 6th tier — **Efsane/Efsanevi (Legendary)** — was added later in the game's life, which proved to be a pivotal and destructive addition (see P2W Analysis).

### Card Upgrade/Evolution System
- **Combination System**: Players combined lower-value cards to create better players
- **Year Matching Requirement**: To upgrade a player card, you must use a card from the same year of that footballer
- **Upgrade Slots**: Main card slot + 5 additional slots for feeding extra cards to increase upgrade success percentage
- **Probability-Based**: Upgrades were not guaranteed — adding more cards increased success chance
- **Collection System (Koleksiyon)**: Completing a full set of unused cards from the same year/team yielded a Rare Roster or Player Card reward

### Technical Staff Cards
- Coaches could lead team to victory independently in some cases
- Applying a coach's recommended tactics increased player win rate by 1-5%
- Also divided into 5 tiers like player cards

### Equipment Cards
- Enhanced player abilities
- Could be purchased or won through gameplay
- Also tier-based

### Emblems
- Divided into 5 levels
- Front-facing stats: Offensive, Defensive, Power, Speed, Technique, Mind

## 4. MONETIZATION

### Premium Currency: JoyPara (JP)
JoyPara was the premium currency used across all Joygame titles (including Wolfteam, etc.), not exclusive to Goley.

### JoyPara Pricing (Historical TL prices)
| Package | Price (TL) |
|---------|-----------|
| 5,000 JP | 5.85 TL |
| 10,000 JP | 11.35 TL |
| 20,000 JP | 22.45 TL |

*Note: These were the prices when TL/USD rates were much lower. As the Turkish Lira depreciated significantly against the dollar (a key factor in the game's financial collapse), the real cost to Netmarble of maintaining servers increased while Turkish players' purchasing power decreased.*

### Card Pack Pricing (in JoyPara)
| Pack Type | 1x | 5x | 10x | 20x |
|-----------|-----|-----|------|------|
| Advanced Lucky Card Pack | 1,500 JP | 6,000 JP | 9,400 JP | 16,800 JP |
| Super Position Pack | 3,400 JP | 13,600 JP | 23,800 JP | N/A |
| Expert Combine Card Pack | 2,400 JP | 8,900 JP | 15,900 JP | N/A |
| Super League Pack (per league) | 3,400 JP | 13,600 JP | 27,200 JP | N/A |

### Legendary Card Packs (Late-Game Addition)
- **Legendary Edson P. (Pele) Pack**: Premium pricing, contained chance-based drops of Super, Expert, Rare cards
- **Legendary Eric C. (Cantona) Pack**: Similar structure
- These packs had extremely high prices and low odds for top-tier cards

### VIP System
- Monthly VIP rewards based on spending
- Players earned rewards on the first day of the following month based on JoyPara spent in the previous month
- Created a "spend more to earn more" feedback loop
- Two-tier reward structure based on spending thresholds

### Monetization Channels
- Direct JoyPara purchase via E-Pin codes (sold on Hepsiburada, Ekopay, OyunFor, OyunAlisveris, Trendyol)
- In-game card pack purchases
- Equipment and emblem purchases
- VIP tier spending incentives

### Value Perception Issues
- Players who spent 14,000+ JoyPara reported not receiving promised items
- Payment processing failures documented on Sikayetvar
- No refund system when game shut down — all invested money lost
- Customer support described as "non-functional, using standard copy-paste responses"

## 5. PAY-TO-WIN ANALYSIS (CRITICAL SECTION)

### How Pay-to-Win Was Goley? — SEVERELY P2W

Goley's monetization evolved from a relatively balanced free-to-play model into one of the most destructive P2W spirals documented in the Turkish gaming market. The P2W mechanics were the **primary cause** of the game's death.

### Phase 1: Early Days — Balanced F2P (2013-2014)
- Game launched as genuinely free-to-play
- Marketing explicitly stated: "the first MMO football game which can be played 100% free"
- Card tiers (Normal through Super) were achievable through gameplay
- Paying players had advantages but the gap was manageable
- Daily quests provided currency for progression
- Training matches and collections allowed F2P card acquisition
- **Player sentiment**: Positive, growing community

### Phase 2: Monetization Escalation (2015-2016)
- More powerful card packs introduced at higher prices
- Super-tier cards became increasingly difficult to obtain without paying
- **Power gap widened**: Paying players' squads significantly outperformed free players
- New pack types with better stats released regularly, devaluing older cards
- Equipment cards and emblems created additional stat layers favoring spenders
- Technical staff cards at higher tiers locked behind premium purchases
- **Player sentiment**: Growing complaints, but still playable

### Phase 3: Legendary Card Introduction — The Breaking Point (2016-2017)
- **CRITICAL MISTAKE**: Introduction of "Efsane/Efsanevi" (Legendary) tier cards
- Legendary cards were FAR more powerful than existing Super-tier cards
- Available primarily through expensive premium packs
- Named legendary packs (Edson P./Pele, Eric C./Cantona) at extreme prices
- Created an insurmountable gap between paying and free players
- **Community consensus**: "Legendary cards ruined the game" (Efsane kartlar oyunu mahvetti)
- Free players couldn't compete at all in ranked modes
- **Player sentiment**: Mass exodus begins

### Phase 4: Desperation & Collapse (2017-2018)
- Netmarble/Joygame attempted to retain players by **distributing powerful cards for free**
- This created a NEW problem: veteran paying players felt cheated (their purchased cards devalued)
- Revenue plummeted because players no longer needed to pay
- Simultaneously, **massive gap between new and old players' rosters** emerged
- New players joining found themselves hopelessly outmatched regardless of spending
- New player retention dropped to near zero
- **Player sentiment**: Anger, abandonment, complaints on Sikayetvar

### Specific P2W Mechanics That Destroyed the Game

#### 1. Card Tier Power Creep
- Each new tier made the previous one obsolete
- Normal -> Special -> Rare -> Expert -> Super -> Legendary
- Legendary cards had stats so high that a full Legendary team was untouchable by a full Super team
- No balancing mechanism to keep older cards competitive

#### 2. Pack Odds (Gacha)
- Card packs were probability-based with no published odds
- Players reported spending thousands of JoyPara without getting desired cards
- "Lucky" card packs were the primary revenue driver
- The combination of high prices + low odds + essential cards = predatory monetization

#### 3. Matchmaking Failure
- Ranked matchmaking did not adequately account for team power level
- Paying players with Legendary/Super teams matched against free players with Normal/Special teams
- Leader Mode attempted to fix this with point-based matching, but team power still determined outcomes
- No separate brackets for different spending tiers
- Result: Free players experienced constant, demoralizing losses

#### 4. Card Economy Inflation
- Regular release of new, more powerful cards inflated the economy
- Cards purchased months earlier became worthless as better versions released
- No trade system or card recycling that preserved value
- Combine/upgrade system consumed multiple cards but success wasn't guaranteed
- Failed upgrades destroyed invested cards — a double-punishment mechanic

#### 5. VIP Spending Loop
- VIP rewards incentivized monthly spending thresholds
- Created pressure to maintain spending levels to receive monthly bonuses
- Essentially a subscription-like system hidden behind spending rewards

#### 6. Equipment & Emblem Stat Stacking
- Beyond player cards, equipment and emblems added stat bonuses
- Full premium equipment sets created multiplicative advantages
- Free players lacked access to top-tier equipment
- This hidden layer of P2W was less visible but equally impactful

### Timeline of P2W Escalation
```
2013-2014  [||||------]  Mild P2W — Cosmetic + minor advantages
2015       [||||||----]  Moderate P2W — Power gap noticeable
2016       [||||||||--]  Heavy P2W — Legendary cards introduced
2017       [||||||||||]  Maximum P2W — Unplayable for free players
2018       [XXXXXXXXXX]  Collapse — Free card giveaways destroy economy
```

### What Specific Mechanics Drove Players Away?
1. **Unwinnable matches** against whale teams
2. **Card devaluation** — spending money only for cards to become obsolete
3. **Failed upgrades** destroying cards with no compensation
4. **Legendary tier** creating an uncrossable gulf
5. **Lack of content updates** beyond new card packs (no new game modes, maps, etc.)
6. **Rampant cheating** — card copying hacks, exploits (Joygame failed to address)
7. **Customer support failures** — complaints ignored, money lost with no recourse

## 6. SEASON/RESET MECHANICS

### Ranked System
- **Leader Mode** featured weekly ranking periods
- Players competed for top positions based on Leader Points
- Weekly rewards for top-ranked players
- Points appeared to reset or recalculate weekly

### Season/Reset Issues
- **No documented full seasonal reset** (unlike FIFA's FUT season model)
- The game did NOT implement seasonal card resets
- This meant accumulated advantages were permanent
- New players joining in 2016-2017 faced 3+ years of accumulated card advantage from veterans
- **No catch-up mechanics** for new players
- The lack of resets contributed to the insurmountable gap between old and new players

### Impact of No-Reset Model
- Veterans had permanently superior rosters
- No "fresh start" feeling to re-engage lapsed players
- Card power ceiling kept rising without floor resets
- Late-joining players had no path to competitiveness

## 7. PLAYER RETENTION & CHURN

### What Kept Players Initially (Retention Drivers)
1. **Sabri Ugan commentary** — Beloved Turkish commentator created authentic atmosphere
2. **Licensed real players** — Real footballers from top European leagues (2005-2012)
3. **Unique goal celebrations** — Character-specific animations, even fun when conceding
4. **Social features** — Playing with friends, clubs, friendship mode
5. **Turkish language** — Fully localized, rare for quality football games at the time
6. **Free-to-play accessibility** — Low barrier to entry
7. **Card collecting dopamine** — Opening packs, upgrading players, building dream teams
8. **Nostalgia factor** — Chibi-style characters, simple fun gameplay
9. **Community** — Active forums, guides, fan blogs
10. **Daily quests** — Regular engagement hooks with currency rewards

### What Drove Players Away (Churn Factors)
1. **P2W escalation** — The #1 reason by far, cited in every community discussion
2. **Legendary card introduction** — The single most destructive update
3. **Cheating/exploits unaddressed** — Card copying, hacks, bugs not fixed
4. **Card devaluation** — Purchased cards becoming obsolete
5. **Matchmaking imbalance** — Free vs. whale player matches
6. **No meaningful updates** — Game stagnated beyond new card releases
7. **Customer support failures** — Money issues unresolved
8. **Rising competition** — FIFA Ultimate Team, PES mobile, other alternatives improving
9. **Technical issues** — Patch errors, bugs, launcher problems
10. **New player hostility** — Impossible to catch up to veterans

### Community Complaints (Documented)
- **Sikayetvar**: Multiple complaints about payment issues, account closures, undelivered items
- **Technopat**: Threads asking "Why didn't Joygame update Goley?" and "Will Goley come back?"
- **Eksi Sozluk**: Described as fun for casual play but lacking long-term value
- **Donanim Arsivi**: Nostalgic remembrance threads, players mourning the game's decline
- **Joygame Forums**: Bug reports, balance complaints, feature requests (mostly unaddressed)

## 8. STRENGTHS — What Goley Did RIGHT

1. **Authentic Turkish Localization**: Full Turkish UI, menus, and critically, Turkish commentary by Sabri Ugan. This created genuine market differentiation in a sea of English-only football games.

2. **Appealing Art Style**: The noseless chibi characters were charming and distinctive. Players explicitly mentioned finding them sympathetic and endearing. This gave Goley a unique visual identity vs. realistic FIFA/PES.

3. **Goal Celebration System**: Individual celebration animations per player were a standout feature. The community specifically praised how even conceding goals was entertaining — a remarkable design achievement.

4. **Low System Requirements**: As a lightweight client game, it ran on most Turkish computers, maximizing accessibility in a market where gaming PCs were not common.

5. **Free-to-Play Accessibility (Initially)**: The early game was genuinely playable without paying. Daily quests, collections, and training provided progression paths.

6. **Card Collection Depth**: 4 card categories (Players, Staff, Equipment, Emblems) with 5 tiers each provided meaningful collection depth and strategic team-building.

7. **Multiple Game Modes**: 5-7 different modes catered to casual (Friendship) and competitive (Ranked, Leader) players.

8. **Social Infrastructure**: Friend matches, clubs, community forums created social bonds that drove retention.

9. **Collection/Combination System**: The ability to combine unused cards into better ones gave all cards some value (initially).

10. **Market Timing**: Launched when there was no viable free-to-play alternative to FIFA/PES in Turkey, capturing an underserved market.

## 9. WEAKNESSES & MISTAKES — Complete List

### Critical Mistakes (Game-Killing)
1. **Introducing Legendary tier cards** without balancing existing tiers
2. **No published pack odds** — predatory gacha without transparency
3. **Failing to address cheating and card copying exploits** for extended periods
4. **Distributing powerful cards for free as a retention Band-Aid** — destroyed paying player trust AND revenue simultaneously
5. **No matchmaking based on team power** — only rank points, creating mismatches
6. **No seasonal reset mechanism** — permanent advantage accumulation
7. **No catch-up mechanics for new players** — hostile to newcomers
8. **Ignoring customer support** — never responded on Sikayetvar, copy-paste support responses

### Major Mistakes
9. **Power creep without card rebalancing** — each new release made previous purchases worthless
10. **Server costs in USD, revenue in TL** — currency depreciation made the business model unsustainable
11. **No content updates beyond card packs** — no new game modes, features, or gameplay innovation
12. **VIP system encouraging spending addiction** rather than value delivery
13. **Failed upgrade system** — destroying cards on failed upgrades with no safety net
14. **No card trading system** — players couldn't exchange or sell unwanted cards
15. **Mobile version (Football Strike) abandoned** — never properly developed or marketed

### Minor Mistakes
16. **Over-reliance on Netmarble for development** — Joygame as publisher couldn't fix issues independently
17. **No esports or competitive scene development** — missed community-building opportunity
18. **Limited licensed player roster** (2005-2012 only) — became outdated over time
19. **No cross-platform play** — PC, mobile, and Facebook versions were siloed
20. **Failure to evolve gameplay** — same core mechanics for 5 years

## 10. SUSTAINABILITY SCORE & FAILURE ANALYSIS

### Sustainability Score: 2/10

### Why Did Goley Decline and Fail?

The failure of Goley is a textbook case of **monetization destroying a game from the inside out**. The game died from a combination of interconnected failures:

#### Root Cause Chain
```
High card prices
    -> Players resort to cheating/exploits
        -> Developers fail to fix cheating
            -> Developers release even MORE powerful (Legendary) cards to compensate
                -> Free players can't compete at all
                    -> Free player exodus
                        -> Developers give away powerful cards to retain players
                            -> Revenue collapses
                                -> Paying players feel cheated (devaluation)
                                    -> Paying player exodus
                                        -> No revenue to maintain servers
                                            -> TL/USD depreciation makes costs unsustainable
                                                -> Korean servers close first
                                                    -> Turkish servers close
                                                        -> GAME OVER
```

#### The Four Fatal Factors
1. **Economic**: TL depreciation against USD made server costs untenable while reducing Turkish players' purchasing power
2. **Design**: Legendary card tier created an irreparable balance gap
3. **Technical**: Inability to combat cheating and card copying exploits
4. **Strategic**: Panic decision to distribute free powerful cards destroyed both revenue AND game balance simultaneously

#### Comparison to FIFA Ultimate Team
FIFA FUT succeeds where Goley failed because:
- Seasonal resets keep the economy fresh
- New players can compete each season
- Matchmaking considers team rating
- Regular gameplay updates beyond just new cards
- Anti-cheat systems work
- Published pack odds (in many regions)
- Card trading market provides value floor

### Lessons for Competitor Development
1. **NEVER introduce a new card tier** that makes all previous tiers obsolete without a transition plan
2. **Implement seasonal resets** to prevent permanent advantage accumulation
3. **Match by team power**, not just rank, to protect free players
4. **Publish pack odds** for trust and (increasingly) legal compliance
5. **Address cheating immediately** — delay compounds the damage exponentially
6. **Never devalue purchases** — if you give away what people paid for, you lose both revenue AND trust
7. **Diversify content updates** — new cards alone are not enough
8. **Plan for currency fluctuation** if operating in emerging markets
9. **Responsive customer support** is non-negotiable
10. **Catch-up mechanics** for new players are essential for growth

## APPENDIX A: Spiritual Successor — International Soccer Game (ISG)

A community-driven project called **International Soccer Game (ISG)** was announced on Technopat forums (2022-2023) as a spiritual successor to Goley. The developers explicitly stated they would:
- Remove bugs from gameplay
- Balance legendary cards properly
- **Not make it Pay-to-Win** (directly addressing Goley's #1 failure)
- Change team/game names to avoid trademark issues

The existence of this project demonstrates the lasting demand for Goley's core concept, if executed without P2W mechanics.
